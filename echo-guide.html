1. Top Header & Diagnostic Controls

CONTROL 01 • TOP BAR

TT (Tone Test & Audio Loopback)

Push Button • Top Right Header

What it Does (In Plain English)

Tapping TT sends a brief 1-second audio tone through your phone's microphone and speaker. It instantly tests whether your microphone, earpiece, and loud audio speaker are working properly before you head out into the field.

Real-World Field Scenario

Scenario: You just put on heavy tactical gloves and connected a wired 3.5mm throat mic or Bluetooth headset in freezing mountain weather. Before setting off from camp into canyon terrain where there is zero cellular signal, you tap TT. A crisp 1000 Hz beep chimes directly in your ear, confirming that your headset audio pipeline is live and ready for two-way voice transmission without needing another person to answer a radio check.

Technical Specifications & Protocol Architecture

  • Audio Pipeline: Generates a local 1000 Hz sinusoidal test burst through Android's low-latency OpenSL ES / AAudio stream.
  • Loopback Verification: Verifies the audio HAL hardware output buffer and microphone ADC without transmitting RF packets over the mesh socket.
  • Sampling Frequency: 16,000 Hz, 16-bit mono PCM.
CONTROL 02 • TOP BAR

DOC (Offline Field Documentation)

Push Button • Top Right Header

What it Does (In Plain English)

Opens the built-in, completely offline user manual, NATO phonetic alphabet chart, emergency radio protocols, and channel frequency directory right on your screen without requiring any internet connection.

Real-World Field Scenario

Scenario: You need to coordinate with an allied search-and-rescue team who asks to switch to "Sub-Channel 4 Tone 12". You are 20 miles outside of cellular range and cannot Google the tone chart. You tap DOC to open the instant offline guide, review the CTCSS frequency table, and configure your radio to match their frequency in seconds.

Technical Specifications & Protocol Architecture

  • Storage Architecture: Embedded compiled SQLite/Markdown asset bundled within the APK; zero network dependency.
  • Reference Content: Standard VHF/UHF tactical preset tables, CTCSS/DCS sub-audible tone matrices, ITU/NATO phonetic tables, and emergency distress frequency guides.
CONTROL 03 • TOP BAR

INF (Mesh Node Diagnostics & IP Roster)

Push Button • Top Right Header

What it Does (In Plain English)

Shows your device's exact identity on the radio mesh: your assigned IP address (e.g. 192.168.49.1), MAC address, Wi-Fi Direct Group Owner status, connected members, and background socket health.

Real-World Field Scenario

Scenario: You notice audio dropped momentarily during a convoy movement. You tap INF and immediately see that your phone transitioned from Group Member to Autonomous Group Owner, and the connection RSSI is -68 dBm. This confirms you are successfully holding the mesh root and other squad devices can link to your phone as they come back within 100 meters.

Technical Specifications & Protocol Architecture

  • Socket Architecture: UDP Multicast / Unicast streaming over port 42424.
  • Wi-Fi P2P Role: Displays isGroupOwner boolean, local IP lease, and P2P interface state via Android WifiP2pManager.
CONTROL 04 • TOP BAR

CFG (Mesh Configuration & Encryption Key)

Push Button • Top Right Header

What it Does (In Plain English)

Opens the settings panel where you type in your Radio Call Sign (e.g. "EAGLE-1"), pick your Radio Group ID ("TEAM-ALPHA"), and set an AES-256 password so only your team can hear your voice and read your messages.

Real-World Field Scenario

Scenario: You are arriving at a multi-agency wilderness command site. The incident commander says your team is "Squad Sierra". You tap CFG, change your Call Sign to SIERRA-LEAD, enter the squad's shared 8-digit mission code, and tap Save. From that moment, your radio only talks with Sierra squad members, preventing crosstalk with other teams in the same valley.

Technical Specifications & Protocol Architecture

  • Encryption: AES-256-GCM symmetric cryptographic engine. All Opus audio frames and JSON packets are salted and encrypted on-the-fly.
  • Node Identification: 128-bit UUID paired with a human-readable 16-character UTF-8 Call Sign.
CONTROL 05 • QUICK TACTICAL BAR

VOL SPK (Speaker Volume & Routing Mode)

Toggle Button • Top Left

What it Does (In Plain English)

Switches between loud speakerphone mode (so everyone around you can hear incoming calls) and private earpiece mode (so only you can hear incoming voice when holding the phone to your ear). Also lets you set the maximum volume boost up to 85 dB.

Real-World Field Scenario

Scenario: You are on a stealth wildlife tracking patrol at dusk. A loud radio blast would scatter the animals. You tap VOL SPK to toggle from Speaker to Earpiece mode. Now, incoming squad transmissions play only through your phone's discreet ear receiver, keeping total tactical noise discipline.

Technical Specifications & Protocol Architecture

  • Audio Routing: Invokes AudioManager.setSpeakerphoneOn(boolean) and sets STREAM_VOICE_CALL routing parameters.
  • Dynamic Gain: Software AGC (Automatic Gain Control) limiter preventing acoustic distortion up to 85 dB SPL output.
CONTROL 06 • QUICK TACTICAL BAR

CH 06 (Quick Channel Preset Switcher)

Quick Selector • Tactical Header

What it Does (In Plain English)

Quickly switches between 10 standard preset channels (Channel 01 through Channel 10). Tapping it cycles through channels so everyone on the team can get on the same frequency with a single touch.

Real-World Field Scenario

Scenario: Team leader announces over Channel 1: "All units, switch to Channel 6 for fire perimeter operations." You tap the CH badge directly on screen; it immediately switches from CH 01 to CH 06, re-tuning your radio to the new channel without opening any complex settings menus.

Technical Specifications & Protocol Architecture

  • Preset Architecture: 10 pre-allocated frequency slots mapped to standardized frequency pairs and UDP multicast channel tags.
  • Switching Latency: Sub-5ms socket retuning with immediate packet filter update.
CONTROL 07 • QUICK TACTICAL BAR

TORCH (Tactical Flashlight & Strobe)

Toggle Button • Tactical Header

What it Does (In Plain English)

Turns your phone's rear LED camera flash into an instant tactical flashlight. Tapping it turns the light ON or OFF immediately, without having to swipe down the Android notification shade.

Real-World Field Scenario

Scenario: You are navigating a rocky trail at night and drop your compass into the brush. While holding your walkie-talkie open in one hand, you tap TORCH with your thumb. The high-power LED illuminates the ground immediately, allowing you to find your gear without closing the radio screen.

Technical Specifications & Protocol Architecture

  • Hardware Interface: Android CameraManager.setTorchMode(cameraId, boolean) native API.
  • Low Power Profile: Bypasses camera sensor preview buffers to conserve battery during extended search operations.
CONTROL 08 • QUICK TACTICAL BAR

SOS (Slide-to-Activate Emergency Distress Beacon)

Slide Control • Top Right

What it Does (In Plain English)

A safety slider that sends an urgent emergency distress beacon across all mesh channels. Sliding the red knob to the right overrides all radio traffic and broadcasts an emergency alert with your exact GPS coordinates and battery level to everyone within range.

Real-World Field Scenario

Scenario: A team member slips down a steep ravine and fractures an ankle. In extreme pain and unable to climb up, they slide the red SOS switch. Instantly, every other teammate's phone sounds a high-priority distress alarm, automatically pins the injured member's coordinates on their maps, and highlights the beacon in red.

Technical Specifications & Protocol Architecture

  • Broadcast Priority: Level-0 Critical Interrupt. Bypasses channel squelch and forces receiver speaker output even if silenced.
  • Payload Telemetry: High-precision WGS-84 coordinates, barometric altitude, timestamp, and battery percentage transmitted every 1.5 seconds over mesh flood.

2. VFO & Dual Watch Control Bar

CONTROL 09 • VFO BAR

≡ MENU (Full Radio Parameters)

Push Button • Lower Bar Left

What it Does (In Plain English)

Opens the in-depth radio settings menu, allowing you to configure Squelch sensitivity (blocking static noise), Microphone Gain, Roger Beep, and VOX hands-free voice activation.

Real-World Field Scenario

Scenario: You are driving an open-top off-road vehicle with loud wind noise. The wind keeps triggering the microphone. You tap ≡ MENU, adjust the Squelch threshold from 2 to 6, and turn on the Roger Beep tone so teammates know when you have finished talking despite engine roar.

Technical Specifications & Protocol Architecture

  • Configurable Parameters: Squelch (SQL 0–9), VOX threshold (-40 dBFS to -12 dBFS), Roger Beep (1750 Hz 80ms tone), Mic Preamp gain (0 to +18 dB).
CONTROL 10 & 11 • VFO BAR

◀ CH & CH ▶ (Channel Step Stepper)

Directional Steppers • Center Control Bar

What it Does (In Plain English)

Steps your active radio channel up or down one by one, like the tuning dial on a handheld walkie-talkie.

Real-World Field Scenario

Scenario: You want to check if the camp generator team is active on Channel 07 while you are currently on Channel 06. You tap CH ▶ once to hop to CH 07, listen for traffic, and then tap ◀ CH to return to your primary team channel.

Technical Specifications & Protocol Architecture

  • Channel Ring: Modulo-10 index ring cycling seamlessly across channels 01 through 10.
  • Audio Sidetone: Emits a subtle 40ms click confirmation on step change.
CONTROL 12 • VFO BAR

⇄ VFO A / VFO B (Active VFO Toggle)

Toggle Button • Right Center

What it Does (In Plain English)

EXOS ECHO has two independent radio tuners built-in: VFO A (Primary) and VFO B (Secondary). Tapping ⇄ VFO switches which channel you transmit on when you press the Hold-to-Talk button.

Real-World Field Scenario

Scenario: You have VFO A set to Channel 06 (your local hiking group) and VFO B set to Channel 01 (the basecamp dispatch). While walking, you talk to your group on VFO A. Basecamp calls out with a weather bulletin. You tap ⇄ VFO to immediately switch your transmit focus to VFO B, acknowledge the basecamp dispatcher, and tap it again to return to your local group.

Technical Specifications & Protocol Architecture

  • Dual VFO Architecture: Emulates standard commercial dual-watch transceivers (Baofeng, Yaesu, Icom) in pure software.
  • State Management: Maintains separate channel frequencies, encryption sub-keys, and volume balances for VFO A and VFO B concurrently.
CONTROL 13 • VFO BAR

TDR (True Dual Receive / Dual Watch)

Toggle Button • Far Right

What it Does (In Plain English)

Turns ON or OFF the ability to listen to TWO channels at the same time. When TDR is ON, you will hear incoming voice calls from both VFO A and VFO B, so you never miss an important message.

Real-World Field Scenario

Scenario: You are leading a group of 12 hikers. Scouts are on Channel 03 while safety officers are on Channel 09. You enable TDR: ON. When scouts call about trail hazards on CH 03, you hear them. Five minutes later, when the medical officer checks in on CH 09, you hear that too, without ever having to touch a knob.

Technical Specifications & Protocol Architecture

  • Dual Audio Demuxing: Ingests dual UDP multicast audio streams and runs simultaneous Opus decoders with audio ducking: if VFO A receives voice, VFO B volume is automatically ducked by 12 dB to maintain priority clarity.

3. PTT Engine & Tactical Keypad

CONTROL 14 • CORE TRANSMITTER

HOLD TO TALK (Push-To-Talk Voice Bar)

Momentary Hold Bar • Screen Center

What it Does (In Plain English)

The primary transmission button. Press and hold this bar while speaking into your phone's microphone. Your voice is instantly digitized, encrypted, and streamed across the mesh to all teammates on your active channel. Release the bar when you are done speaking to return to listening.

Real-World Field Scenario

Scenario: You spot a fast-moving storm cloud approaching the ridge. You press and hold the large cyan HOLD TO TALK bar with your thumb, say: "All units, heavy rain approaching the saddle in 10 minutes, put on rain gear," and let go. The transmission terminates with a brief confirmation chime and your radio instantly returns to standby.

Technical Specifications & Protocol Architecture

  • Codec Engine: Native Opus interactive audio codec operating at 16 kHz sampling rate, 20ms frame packetization, and dynamic 24 kbps variable bitrate.
  • Transport Latency: Sub-80ms end-to-end voice latency over direct Wi-Fi P2P 802.11 sockets.
  • Floor Control: Distributed token bucket algorithm preventing voice packet collisions.
CONTROL 15 • TACTICAL KEYPAD

* LOCK (Keypad Lock / Accidental Press Guard)

Toggle Button • Lower Left Keypad

What it Does (In Plain English)

Locks the screen controls so that putting your phone into your pocket, backpack, or tactical vest pouch will not accidentally change channels or trigger unintentional button presses.

Real-World Field Scenario

Scenario: You are bushwhacking through dense brush and sliding down rocky slopes. You tap and hold * LOCK. A lock icon appears on screen. Now, branches rubbing against your phone's glass cannot bump you off your squad's channel. When you need to talk, a simple long-press unlocks it.

Technical Specifications & Protocol Architecture

  • Touch Interceptor: Consumes all Android window touch dispatch events except the designated unlock gesture, preventing accidental input state corruption.

Navigation Tab • Bottom Bar Left

The primary command view with the full radio dial, channel display, volume meters, and Push-to-Talk button.

Real-World Field Scenario

Scenario: During active trail patrol, keep this tab open for fast, instant voice communication with your team.

Technical Specifications & Protocol Architecture

TAB 02 • MESH ROSTER

TEAM (Mesh Member Roster & Vital Stats)

Navigation Tab • Bottom Bar Center-Left

What it Does (In Plain English)

Shows a live list of every team member currently connected to the mesh network, their Call Sign, signal strength bar, battery percentage, and time since their last transmission.

Real-World Field Scenario

Scenario: You are the expedition leader. At 18:00 hours, you tap the TEAM tab to do a roll call. You see all 6 members listed in green with strong signal, and notice member "SCOUT-2" has 14% battery remaining. You radio them to swap to their backup battery pack before nightfall.

Technical Specifications & Protocol Architecture

  • Heartbeat Telemetry: Periodic 5-second UDP beacons broadcasting node UUID, battery level, RSSI, and active channel. Nodes automatically transition to "OFFLINE" after 20 seconds of silence.
TAB 03 • TACTICAL CARTOGRAPHY

MAP (Tactical Mesh Radar & Member Locations)

Navigation Tab • Bottom Bar Center-Right

What it Does (In Plain English)

Displays an offline tactical map showing the real-time GPS locations of all teammates on your mesh network, your distance to them, and any active SOS distress beacons.

Real-World Field Scenario

Scenario: Heavy mountain fog rolls in, reducing visibility to less than 10 meters. You lose visual contact with your partner. You open the MAP tab; your partner's icon appears 85 meters to your northeast at bearing 042°. You walk straight toward their icon and rejoin the team without shouting or getting lost.

Technical Specifications & Protocol Architecture

  • Coordinate Mesh Sync: Lightweight JSON coordinate packets broadcast over the mesh layer; renders using high-contrast offline vector cartography with zero cellular data required.
TAB 04 • ENCRYPTED CHAT

MSGS (Peer-to-Peer Encrypted Text Messaging)

Navigation Tab • Bottom Bar Right

What it Does (In Plain English)

Lets you send silent, encrypted text messages, GPS coordinates, and quick tactical status codes (e.g. "AT OBJECTIVE", "HOLDING POSITION") over the mesh when you cannot speak aloud.

Real-World Field Scenario

Scenario: You are monitoring wildlife or need silent communication during an operation where noise is strictly prohibited. You open the MSGS tab and type: "Sighted target at ridge waypoint A2, holding position." Every team member receives the message on their screen silently, with zero acoustic noise emitted.

Technical Specifications & Protocol Architecture

  • Store & Forward: Ephemeral mesh packet routing with delivery acknowledgments (ACK). All text payloads are encrypted with AES-256-GCM.
