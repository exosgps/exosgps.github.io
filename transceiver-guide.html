1. Primary Mode Selection Cards

MODE 01 • SENSOR BROADCASTER

Transmitter Mode (Broadcast GPS & Compass)

Primary Mode Card • Top Left

What it Does (In Plain English)

Turns your Android phone into an external Bluetooth GPS antenna and electronic compass for other computers. When active, your phone sends its raw GPS satellite coordinates and True Heading ($GPHDT) to your Windows laptop or tablet over Bluetooth.

Real-World Field Scenario

Scenario: You are navigating a sailboat or field vehicle using a rugged Windows laptop running OpenCPN or QGIS. The laptop has no built-in GPS or compass hardware. You open EXOS Transceiver on your Android phone, select Transmitter Mode, and place the phone near the dashboard window. The laptop connects to the phone over Bluetooth and receives high-precision position and heading updates at 1 Hz, turning the laptop into a marine navigation chartplotter.

Technical Specifications & Protocol Architecture

  • Bluetooth SPP Server: Listens on RFCOMM channel 1 with standard Serial Port Profile UUID 00001101-0000-1000-8000-00805F9B34FB.
  • NMEA Protocol: Transmits standard NMEA 0183 v4.10 sentences: $GPGGA, $GPRMC, $GPGSA, $GPGSV, and true heading $GPHDT.
MODE 02 • EXTERNAL RECEIVER BRIDGE

Receiver Mode (Bridge External Hardware GPS)

Primary Mode Card • Top Right

What it Does (In Plain English)

Connects your phone to an external high-accuracy GPS puck (like a professional survey antenna or ESP32 RTK GPS). Your phone receives survey-grade position data and replaces its internal phone GPS so Google Maps or ATAK benefits from sub-meter accuracy.

Real-World Field Scenario

Scenario: You are performing forestry surveying where heavy tree canopy blocks consumer phone GPS signals. You carry an external helical antenna on your survey pole connected to an ESP32 GNSS receiver. In EXOS Transceiver, you select Receiver Mode, pair with the survey pole, and activate Mock Location. Your surveying GIS app now maps trees with centimeter precision.

Technical Specifications & Protocol Architecture

  • Baud Rate Support: Connects to external Bluetooth serial devices at 4800, 9600, 38400, 57600, 115200, and 230400 bps.
  • Parser Engine: Asynchronous circular buffer with real-time XOR checksum verification and RTK differential fix parsing (GGA Fix Quality 4 & 5).

3. Transmitter Mode Controls & Sensor Stream

TRANSMISSION TOPOLOGY • SECTION 2

Transmission Mode: BROADCAST vs P2P DEDICATED

Topology Selector • Transmitter Controls

What it Does (In Plain English)

Lets you choose between sharing your GPS coordinates with multiple nearby computers at once (Broadcast Mode) or creating a locked, high-speed one-to-one link to a single laptop (P2P Dedicated Mode).

Real-World Field Scenario

Scenario: In a mobile command vehicle, three officers on three separate tablets need live coordinates from the vehicle's roof-mounted smartphone. Setting the mode to BROADCAST allows all three tablets to connect simultaneously, receiving synchronized GPS telemetry.

Technical Specifications & Protocol Architecture

  • Broadcast Mode: Multi-client RFCOMM socket server allowing up to 7 concurrent paired Bluetooth clients with dynamic disconnect controls.
  • P2P Dedicated Mode: Exclusive single-client channel with maximum throughput buffer utilization and reduced latency (< 15 ms).
TRANSMITTER ENGINE • PRIMARY ACTION

START BROADCASTING / STOP BROADCASTING

Primary Action Button • Transmitter Screen

What it Does (In Plain English)

The main push button that turns on the satellite transmitter engine. Once tapped, your phone starts streaming live GPS coordinates and compass orientation over Bluetooth.

Real-World Field Scenario

Scenario: You mount your phone to your boat's helm station, tap START BROADCASTING, and walk down to the cabin. The button pulses green to indicate active transmission. Your laptop in the cabin instantly receives the stream and begins charting your course.

Technical Specifications & Protocol Architecture

  • Service Lifecycle: Starts TransmitterService as an Android Foreground Service with continuous PARTIAL_WAKE_LOCK.
  • Sensor Fusion: Fuses Android GNSS measurements with geomagnetic vector calculations to output continuous $GPGGA, $GPRMC, $GPGSA, $GPGSV, and $GPHDT.
TELEMETRY MONITOR • SENSOR STATUS

Local GPS Data & Hardware Status Cards

Telemetry Cards • Transmitter Screen

What it Does (In Plain English)

Shows how many satellites are currently locked, your positioning accuracy in meters, and whether your electronic compass sensor is calibrated and active.

Real-World Field Scenario

Scenario: Before starting a boat run, you check the Hardware Status card. It shows "GPS Satellite Lock: Active" with 18 satellites locked and "Compass: Active". You know your navigation telemetry is rock-solid before untying from the dock.

Technical Specifications & Protocol Architecture

  • Reads raw NMEA constellation data via GnssStatus.Callback and reports horizontal dilution of precision (HDOP), position error margin (RMS), and 3-axis compass calibration quality.

4. Receiver Mode & Smart Guided Mock Location

RECEIVER BRIDGE • MOCK LOCATION ENGINE

Smart Guided Mock Location Provider

System Switch • Receiver Screen

What it Does (In Plain English)

Injects satellite coordinates from an external GPS antenna directly into Android's system location provider. Every navigation app on your phone (including Google Maps, Waze, and ATAK) now uses the external antenna automatically.

Real-World Field Scenario

Scenario: You have an Android tablet without built-in GPS. You pair it to your external GPS antenna using EXOS Transceiver in Receiver Mode and turn on Smart Guided Mock Location. Google Maps immediately shows your blue navigation dot and provides full turn-by-turn guidance.

Technical Specifications & Protocol Architecture

  • Mock Provider: Utilizes Android LocationManager.setTestProviderLocation() on LocationManager.GPS_PROVIDER.
  • Guided Setup: Automatically detects if Developer Options mock location app is selected and provides deep links to Android settings if configuration is required.
